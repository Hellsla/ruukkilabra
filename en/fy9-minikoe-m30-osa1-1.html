FY9Mini exam 30 min: Part I (1)

FY9 · Exam mode

Mini exam 30 min: Part I (1)

Time 30 min, at most 10 points. Hints, feedback and model answers open when you end the exam.

  • Answer 5 of the 6 items: choose them before you answer.

1. FY1 · 1/3 · Kind of error 2 points

A timekeeper measures with a stopwatch the time a ball takes to roll along a channel and starts the watch a little late every time. The measurement is repeated ten times, and the mean of the results is calculated.Which statement is correct?

  • The length of the delays may vary, but they always shorten the time. So the mean is too short as well. Repeating reduces only the random variation. Review this in the chapter Measuring and the reliability of a measurement.

  • A random error varies in both directions. Here the watch always starts late, so the error always has the same direction, that is it is systematic, and the mean does not remove it. Review this in the chapter Measuring and the reliability of a measurement.

  • Correct. A late start shortens every time in the same direction, so the mean is too small as well. Repeating reduces only the random error. Review this in the chapter Measuring and the reliability of a measurement.

  • A gross error is a single clear mistake, such as a misread reading. A late start every time is a systematic error, corrected by changing the method, for example with light gates. Review this in the chapter Measuring and the reliability of a measurement.

2. FY2 · 2/3 · The fireplace 2 points

In a fireplace $2.5\,\text{kg}$ of birch logs are burnt, with a heating value of $15\,\text{MJ/kg}$. The efficiency of the fireplace is $70\,\%$: that is the share of the energy released in burning that heats the room.

How much energy does the fireplace give to the room? Answer in megajoules.

MJ
Hint 1: a guiding question

How much energy does the wood release when it burns?

Hint 2: what is the connection

How large a share of it stays in the room? Multiply by the efficiency.

Hint 3: an intermediate step

$$ \begin{aligned} E_\text{useful} &= 0.70 \cdot 15\,\text{MJ/kg} \cdot 2.5\,\text{kg} \end{aligned} $$$$ \begin{aligned} E_\text{useful} &= 0.70 \cdot 15\,\text{MJ/kg} \\ &\cdot 2.5\,\text{kg} \end{aligned} $$

Show the solution

Given

mass of the fuel $m = 2.5\,\text{kg}$, heating value $H = 15\,\text{MJ/kg}$, efficiency $\eta = 70\,\% = 0.70$

Find

the energy transferred to the room $E_\text{useful}$ in megajoules

Principle

When it burns, the wood releases the energy $E = Hm$. The efficiency tells how large a share of it goes to the useful purpose, here heating the room; the rest goes out with the flue gases.

Formula

$E = Hm$ and $\eta = \dfrac{E_\text{useful}}{E}$, so $E_\text{useful} = \eta H m$

Conversions

The percentage as a decimal: $70\,\% = 0.70$. The heating value is already in the unit $\text{MJ/kg}$, so the result comes out in megajoules.

Substitution

$$ \begin{aligned} E_\text{useful} &= 0.70 \cdot 15\,\text{MJ/kg} \cdot 2.5\,\text{kg} \\ &= 26.25\,\text{MJ} \end{aligned} $$$$ \begin{aligned} E_\text{useful} &= 0.70 \cdot 15\,\text{MJ/kg} \\ &\cdot 2.5\,\text{kg} \\ &= 26.25\,\text{MJ} \end{aligned} $$

Answer

$E_\text{useful} \approx 26\,\text{MJ}$

Two significant figures like the given values.

Reasonableness

In all, the wood releases $15 \cdot 2.5\,\text{MJ} \approx 38\,\text{MJ}$, and the useful part is less, as it should be. $26\,\text{MJ}$ is about $7\,\text{kWh}$, which matches an electric heater switched on for a few hours.

3. FY4 · 2/3 · Collision 2 points

A heavy lorry and a small car collide.How does the force the lorry exerts on the car compare with the force the car exerts on the lorry during the collision?

  • Speed does not make the forces of a pair unequal. The forces of an interaction are equal in size and opposite in direction whatever the state of motion. Review this in the chapter Interaction and force: Newton's first and third laws.

  • Correct. The forces are the force pair of the same interaction, so by Newton's third law they are equal in size. The same force gives a smaller mass a larger acceleration. Review this in the chapter Interaction and force: Newton's first and third laws.

  • The damage tells about the acceleration and the structure, not about a difference in the forces. The car gets the same force, but its smaller mass accelerates more. Review this in the chapter Interaction and force: Newton's first and third laws.

  • Mass does not make the forces of an interaction unequal. A force pair is always equal in size; the mass affects only how much each one accelerates. Review this in the chapter Interaction and force: Newton's first and third laws.

4. A train passes the platform 2 points

A passenger standing on a platform hears the horn of a passing train at the frequency $562\,\text{Hz}$ while the train approaches and at the frequency $478\,\text{Hz}$ after the train has passed. The train runs at a constant speed, the air is still, and the speed of sound is $340\,\text{m/s}$.

What is the speed of the train?

m/s
Hint 1: a guiding question

Both heard frequencies involve the same unknown frequency $f_0$ of the horn. How can you remove it from the equations?

Hint 2: what is the connection

While approaching $f_1 = f_0 \cdot \tfrac{v}{v - v_\text{L}}$ and while moving away $f_2 = f_0 \cdot \tfrac{v}{v + v_\text{L}}$. Divide one equation by the other.

Hint 3: an intermediate step

$\tfrac{f_1}{f_2} = \tfrac{v + v_\text{L}}{v - v_\text{L}}$, which gives $v_\text{L} = v \cdot \tfrac{f_1 - f_2}{f_1 + f_2}$.

Show the solution

Given

frequency heard while approaching $f_1 = 562\,\text{Hz}$, heard while moving away $f_2 = 478\,\text{Hz}$, speed of sound $v = 340\,\text{m/s}$; the listener is at rest

Find

the speed of the train $v_\text{L}$ in $\text{m/s}$

Principle

The source moves and the listener is at rest, so in front of the source the wavelength becomes shorter and behind it longer: the Doppler formula for a moving source is used, with the minus sign while approaching and the plus sign while moving away (chapter Sound as a wave: pitch, timbre and the Doppler effect). The horn's own frequency $f_0$ is not known, but it is the same in both, so it cancels when one equation is divided by the other.

Formula

$f_1$$= f_0 \cdot \dfrac{v}{v - v_\text{L}}$
$f_2$$= f_0 \cdot \dfrac{v}{v + v_\text{L}}$

so

$\dfrac{f_1}{f_2}$$= \dfrac{v + v_\text{L}}{v - v_\text{L}}$

which gives

$f_1 v - f_1 v_\text{L}$$= f_2 v + f_2 v_\text{L}$
$v_\text{L}$$= v \cdot \dfrac{f_1 - f_2}{f_1 + f_2}$

Conversions

The quantities are in base units, so no conversions are needed; finally the speed is compared in the unit $\text{km/h}$.

Substitution

$v_\text{L}$$= 340\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} \cdot \dfrac{562\,\text{Hz} - 478\,\text{Hz}}{562\,\text{Hz} + 478\,\text{Hz}}$
$= 340\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} \cdot \dfrac{84\,\text{Hz}}{1040\,\text{Hz}}$
$= 27.46\,\text{m/s}$

Answer

$v_\text{L} \approx 27\,\text{m/s}$

Two significant figures, because the result depends on the difference of the frequencies $84\,\text{Hz}$.

Reasonableness

$27.46\,\text{m/s}$$= 27.46 \cdot \tfrac{3600\,\text{s/h}}{1000\,\text{m/km}}$
$\approx 99\,\text{km/h}$

an ordinary speed for a train near a station. The horn's own frequency is

$f_0$$= f_1 \cdot \tfrac{v - v_\text{L}}{v}$
$= 562\,\text{Hz} \cdot \tfrac{312.5}{340}$
$\approx 517\,\text{Hz}$

and it lies between the heard frequencies, as it should.

5. FY6 · 2/3 · Ohm's law 2 points

The voltage across a resistor is raised from $3.0\,\text{V}$ to $6.0\,\text{V}$. The temperature of the resistor stays the same, so its resistance is constant.How does the current through the resistor change?

  • Voltage does not resist current; it drives the current round the circuit. What resists is the resistance, and when it is constant the current grows with the voltage: $I = \tfrac{U}{R}$. Review this in the chapter Resistance and Ohm's law.

  • What is proportional to the square of the voltage is the power of the resistor $P = \tfrac{U^2}{R}$, not the current. The current is proportional to the voltage. Review this in the chapter Resistance and Ohm's law.

  • Correct. By Ohm's law $U = RI$, at a constant resistance the current is proportional to the voltage: $I = \tfrac{U}{R}$. Review this in the chapter Resistance and Ohm's law.

  • The current is set by the voltage and the resistance of the circuit, not by the source alone. When the voltage doubles and the resistance stays the same, the current doubles. Review this in the chapter Resistance and Ohm's law.

6. Fusion of two deuterium nuclei 2 points

In a fusion experiment two deuterium nuclei join: ${}^{2}_{1}\text{H} + {}^{2}_{1}\text{H} \to {}^{3}_{2}\text{He} + {}^{1}_{0}\text{n}$. The atomic masses are ${}^{2}\text{H}$: $2.014102\,\text{u}$, ${}^{3}\text{He}$: $3.016029\,\text{u}$ and the neutron $1.008665\,\text{u}$. How much energy does one reaction release?

MeV
Hint 1: a guiding question

Where does the released energy come from? Compare the masses of the reactants and the products.

Hint 2: what is the connection

$Q = (m_\text{initial} - m_\text{final})\,c^2$. Atomic masses can be used, because there are two electrons on both sides.

Hint 3: an intermediate step

Substitute the mass difference in atomic mass units and $1\,\text{u} = 931.5\,\tfrac{\text{MeV}}{c^2}$.

Show the solution

Given

$m({}^{2}\text{H}) = 2.014102\,\text{u}$, $m({}^{3}\text{He}) = 3.016029\,\text{u}$, $m_\text{n} = 1.008665\,\text{u}$, $1\,\text{u} = 931.5\,\tfrac{\text{MeV}}{c^2}$

Find

the energy $Q$ released by one reaction in $\text{MeV}$

Principle

In a nuclear reaction the mass number and the charge number are conserved, but the mass is not: the products are lighter, and the energy corresponding to the mass difference is released as kinetic energy of the products (chapters Mass defect and nuclear binding energy and Fission, fusion and nuclear power). Atomic masses can be used, because there are two electrons on each side.

Formula

$Q$$= (m_\text{initial} - m_\text{final})\,c^2$
$= \left(2m({}^{2}\text{H}) - m({}^{3}\text{He}) - m_\text{n}\right)c^2$

Conversions

The mass difference is substituted in atomic mass units, and $1\,\text{u} = 931.5\,\tfrac{\text{MeV}}{c^2}$ is substituted for the unit, so that $c^2$ cancels.

Substitution

$$ \begin{aligned} \Delta m &= 2 \cdot 2.014102\,\text{u} - 3.016029\,\text{u} - 1.008665\,\text{u} \\ &= 0.003510\,\text{u}\text{,} \end{aligned} $$$$ \begin{aligned} \Delta m &= 2 \cdot 2.014102\,\text{u} \\ &- 3.016029\,\text{u} \\ &- 1.008665\,\text{u} \\ &= 0.003510\,\text{u}\text{,} \end{aligned} $$ so

$Q$$= 0.003510 \cdot 931.5\,\tfrac{\text{MeV}}{c^2} \cdot c^2$
$= 3.270\,\text{MeV}$

Answer

$Q \approx 3.27\,\text{MeV}$

Three significant figures, because the mass difference $0.003510\,\text{u}$ is known to four figures and the conversion factor $931.5$ to four.

Reasonableness

The mass decreases by only about $0.09\,\%$, which is typical of nuclear reactions. The result is smaller than the $17.6\,\text{MeV}$ of deuterium–tritium fusion, because helium-3 is much less tightly bound than helium-4.
