FY9 · Exam mode

Mini exam 30 min: The Exam and Experimental Work

Time 30 min, at most 10 points. Hints, feedback and model answers open when you end the exam.

1. Command word 2 points

A problem gives a table of the extension of a spring under different loads and asks you to determine the spring constant graphically.Which answer meets the task?

  • A slope calculated from two neighbouring measured points magnifies the measurement errors, and it is not a graphical determination. Read the slope from the line fitted to all the points.

  • A zigzag line is not a model, and one point contains the error of one measurement. Fit a straight line to the points and read its slope.

  • Calculating row by row is not a graphical determination, and it does not reveal an outlying point or where the line crosses the axis. Draw the graph and read the slope from the fit.

  • Correct. Determining graphically means that the result is read from the line fitted to the graph and the reading is shown: two points of the line, or the equation of the program's fit.

Review drawing a graph and the slope in the chapter Graphing and fitting a straight line.

2. FY1 · 3/3 · Density 2 points

A student measures the mass of a metal piece as $54.0\,\text{g}$ and, by immersion, its volume as $20.0\,\text{cm}^3$. Calculate the density of the piece in the unit $\text{kg/m}^3$.

kg/m³
Hint 1: a guiding question

What is the definition of density? Which is divided by which?

Hint 2: what is the connection

Convert first: $1\,\text{g} = 10^{-3}\,\text{kg}$ and $1\,\text{cm}^3 = 10^{-6}\,\text{m}^3$.

Hint 3: an intermediate step
$\rho$$= \dfrac{54.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\text{kg}}{20.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m}^3}$
Show the solution

Given

mass $m = 54.0\,\text{g}$, volume $V = 20.0\,\text{cm}^3$

Find

the density $\rho$ in the unit $\text{kg/m}^3$

Principle

Density is the mass of a substance per unit volume. The piece is of one substance, so its density is its mass divided by its volume.

Formula

$m = \rho V$, so $\rho = \dfrac{m}{V}$

Conversions

$54.0\,\text{g}$$= 54.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\text{kg}$
$20.0\,\text{cm}^3$$= 20.0 \cdot (10^{-2}\,\text{m})^3$
$= 20.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m}^3$

Substitution

$\rho$$= \dfrac{54.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\text{kg}}{20.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m}^3}$
$= 2700\,\text{kg/m}^3$

Answer

$\rho \approx 2.70 \cdot 10^{3}\,\text{kg/m}^3$

Three significant figures like the mass and the volume.

Reasonableness

The result is $2.70\,\text{g/cm}^3$, about $2.7$ times the density of water, which fits a metal. According to the table it is the density of aluminium.

3. Choosing a measuring instrument 2 points

The diameter of a copper wire is about $0.5\,\text{mm}$. With which measuring method is the relative uncertainty of the diameter smallest?

  • The reading uncertainty is about $0.5\,\text{mm}$, as large as the whole diameter: the relative uncertainty is about $100\,\%$. The scale division of the instrument must be much smaller than the quantity measured.

  • The uncertainty is at least of the order of the resolution, about $0.05\,\text{mm}$, or $10\,\%$ of the diameter. A single measurement also does not reveal whether the wire is equally thick everywhere.

  • Correct. The reading uncertainty is about $0.01\,\text{mm}$, or about $2\,\%$ of the diameter, and five measuring points show whether the thickness varies. An instrument is chosen so that its resolution and uncertainty are small compared with the quantity measured.

  • This is a good method if no more precise instrument is available: $20$ diameters make about $10\,\text{mm}$, and the reading uncertainty $0.5\,\text{mm}$ is about $5\,\%$ of that. A micrometer screw gauge still reaches a clearly smaller relative uncertainty.

4. A complete solution 2 points

A local train accelerates uniformly from rest at a station to the speed $72\,\text{km/h}$ in $45\,\text{s}$. Solve it completely in your notebook, and give the acceleration of the train in the unit $\text{m/s}^2$ with the right precision.

m/s²
Hint 1: a guiding question

What is the definition of acceleration when the acceleration is constant?

Hint 2: what is the connection

Convert the speed: $72\,\text{km/h} = \tfrac{72}{3.6}\,\text{m/s}$.

Hint 3: an intermediate step
$a$$= \dfrac{20\,\text{m/s}}{45\,\text{s}}$
Show the solution

Given

initial speed $v_0 = 0$, final speed $v = 72\,\text{km/h}$, time $\Delta t = 45\,\text{s}$; uniform acceleration

Find

the acceleration $a$ in the unit $\text{m/s}^2$

Principle

The train accelerates uniformly, so its acceleration is constant and equal to the change in velocity divided by the time taken.

Formula

$a$$= \dfrac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$
$= \dfrac{v - v_0}{\Delta t}$

Conversions

$72\,\text{km/h}$$= \dfrac{72 \cdot 1000\,\text{m}}{3600\,\text{s}}$
$= 20\,\text{m/s}$

Substitution

$a$$= \dfrac{20\,\text{m/s} - 0}{45\,\text{s}}$
$= 0.4444\,\text{m/s}^2$

Answer

$a \approx 0.44\,\text{m/s}^2$

Two significant figures like the speed $72\,\text{km/h}$ and the time $45\,\text{s}$.

Reasonableness

The acceleration is less than $5\,\%$ of the free-fall acceleration, which fits a train with standing passengers. Check: $0.444\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 45\,\text{s} = 20\,\text{m/s}$.

Review acceleration in the chapter Acceleration and uniformly accelerated motion.

5. A stack of coins 2 points

With a ruler, a student measures the height of a stack of ten identical coins as $18.5\,\text{mm}$. The smallest scale division of the ruler is $1\,\text{mm}$, so the uncertainty of the height of the stack is estimated as $0.5\,\text{mm}$.

How large is the relative uncertainty of the thickness of one coin? Answer in percent.

%
Hint 1: a guiding question

How thick is one coin, and how large is the uncertainty of its thickness, when there are ten coins in the stack?

Hint 2: what is the connection

Both the thickness and its uncertainty are divided by ten: $d = 1.85\,\text{mm}$ and $\Delta d = 0.05\,\text{mm}$.

Hint 3: an intermediate step
$\varepsilon$$= \dfrac{\Delta d}{d} \cdot 100\,\%$
$= \dfrac{0.05\,\text{mm}}{1.85\,\text{mm}} \cdot 100\,\%$
Show the solution

Given

height of the stack $h = 18.5\,\text{mm}$, its uncertainty $\Delta h = 0.5\,\text{mm}$, number of coins $N = 10$

Find

the relative uncertainty $\varepsilon$ of the thickness of one coin in percent

Principle

The coins are identical, so the thickness of one coin is the height of the stack divided by ten. The height of the stack is read once, so the uncertainty of the whole reading is shared by ten coins. The relative uncertainty compares the uncertainty with the result itself.

Formula

$d = \dfrac{h}{N}$ and $\Delta d = \dfrac{\Delta h}{N}$, so

$\varepsilon$$= \dfrac{\Delta d}{d} \cdot 100\,\%$
$= \dfrac{\Delta h}{h} \cdot 100\,\%$

Conversions

Not needed: the millimetres cancel in the ratio.

Substitution

$d$$= 18.5\,\text{mm} : 10$
$= 1.85\,\text{mm}$
$\Delta d$$= 0.5\,\text{mm} : 10$
$= 0.05\,\text{mm}$
$\varepsilon$$= \dfrac{0.05\,\text{mm}}{1.85\,\text{mm}} \cdot 100\,\%$
$= 2.70\,\%$

Answer

$\varepsilon \approx 3\,\%$

($2.7\,\%$). The uncertainty $0.5\,\text{mm}$ is a one-significant-figure estimate, so one figure is enough; two figures are fine for an intermediate result. The thickness of a coin is $(1.85 \pm 0.05)\,\text{mm}$.

Reasonableness

If one coin were measured with the same ruler, the uncertainty would be $0.5\,\text{mm} : 1.85\,\text{mm} \approx 27\,\%$, ten times as large. Measuring a stack reduces the uncertainty for one coin, because the uncertainty of one reading is shared by ten coins.
