FY9 · School-specific course

Physics Matriculation Examination Review

Review, connect and practise for the exam.

This is a school-specific course (Kiviruukki upper secondary school). Its goals and contents come from the school's own plan, not from the national modules FY1–FY8.

Under construction. Units are published as they are completed and checked. The scope and assessment have not been confirmed.

The course reviews the key phenomena, laws and skills of modules FY1–FY8 and practises the task types of the matriculation examination. Every review lesson starts with a starting-level test and ends with original tasks in the style of the matriculation examination, with a model answer and a scoring guide for self-assessment. Each task says which chapter's skills it uses, so you can go straight back to the original chapter to review a weak spot.

Chapters and units

The chapters of the course in order. Each chapter has its own big question, and its units answer it together.

The course has 16 lessons. The first lesson introduces the exam and answering technique, lessons 2–15 review the modules in the order of the exam's contents, and the last lesson is a practice exam.

  1. 1 · The Exam and Experimental Work

    3 lessons
  2. Chapter 1.1The matriculation examination and answering technique

    Why can two answers with the same final result get completely different points?

  3. Chapter 1.2Measuring, uncertainty and graphs

    Why is a result obtained from a straight line fitted to a graph usually more reliable than one calculated from a single measurement?

  4. Chapter 1.3Experimental work and its evaluation

    Why does a good experiment plan already tell, before any measurement, how reliable the result will be?

  5. 2 · Energy and Heat

    3 lessons
  6. Chapter 2.1Energy, power and efficiency

    Why does charging an electric car from a household socket take all night, even though the battery holds less energy than ten litres of petrol?

  7. Chapter 2.2Heat and changes of state

    Why does it take so long to melt snow into drinking water over a campfire, although the snow is only a little below freezing? Which takes more energy: melting it or heating the meltwater to boiling?

  8. Chapter 2.3Pressure, gases and the heat engine

    Why must the pressure of a car's tyres be checked when a hard frost sets in, even though no air has leaked from the tyres?

  9. 3 · Motion and Interaction

    4 lessons
  10. Chapter 3.1Motion as graphs

    Why can you work out the distance between tram stops from the tram's velocity graph, even though the graph does not contain a single metre?

  11. Chapter 3.2Newton's laws and the free-body diagram

    Why can a car set a trailer moving, even though the trailer pulls the car backwards with a force as large as the force with which the car pulls the trailer forwards?

  12. Chapter 3.3Mechanical energy and momentum

    Why do two railway wagons that couple together move on more slowly than before coupling, even though nothing brakes them?

  13. Chapter 3.4Equilibrium, circular motion and gravitation

    Why can a satellite dish be aimed once and left in place, although the television satellite orbits the Earth at a speed of about $3\,\text{km/s}$?

  14. 4 · Oscillations and Waves

    1 lesson
  15. Chapter 4.1Oscillations, waves and sound

    Why can the lifeguard's whistle also be heard under water, and does the sound change on its way from air into water?

  16. 5 · Electricity and Magnetism

    3 lessons
  17. Chapter 5.1Direct-current circuits and electrical energy

    Why does the cottage battery last more than a week for the LED lights alone, but only a day together with the cool box?

  18. Chapter 5.2The electric field and the capacitor

    Why does the spark that jumps from a door handle on a frosty day do no harm, although its voltage is thousands of volts?

  19. Chapter 5.3Magnetism, induction and light

    Why does the electricity for the cottages on an island travel in a submarine cable at a voltage of $20\,\text{kV}$, although the cottages use a voltage of $230\,\text{V}$?

  20. 6 · Modern Physics

    1 lesson
  21. Chapter 6.1Quantum phenomena, the nucleus and radiation

    Why do the bright red grow lights of a greenhouse not damage the DNA of the plants or of the gardener, even though their power is kilowatts, while even weak X-rays can?

  22. 7 · Practice Exam

    1 lesson
  23. Chapter 7.1Practice exam

    Why is it worth doing a six-hour exam once in full before the real exam?

In this course, you will learn to

Working version: the goals will be refined when the course plan is complete.

  • recognise the phenomena in a task and choose a suitable physical model for them
  • write a complete solution with reasoning, units and a check that the result makes sense
  • draw and interpret graphs from measurement data
  • plan an experiment and assess how reliable the measurements are
  • combine ideas from different modules in a new situation
  • assess your own skills and review your weak spots
