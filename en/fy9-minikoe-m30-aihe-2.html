FY9Mini exam 30 min: Energy and Heat

FY9 · Exam mode

Mini exam 30 min: Energy and Heat

Time 30 min, at most 10 points. Hints, feedback and model answers open when you end the exam.

1. Efficiency 2 points

The efficiency of an LED lamp is $35\,\%$: that is the share of the electrical energy it takes in that becomes light.What happens to the rest of the energy?

  • Correct. Energy is always conserved. The efficiency only tells how large a share of the energy ends up in the wanted form; the rest heats the lamp and the room. Review this in the chapter Efficiency.

  • Energy does not disappear in any device. An efficiency below one hundred per cent means that part of the energy turns into something other than the useful form, here internal energy. Review this in the chapter Efficiency.

  • A lamp does not feed energy into the grid. The electrical energy it takes in becomes light and internal energy, and their sum equals the energy taken in. Review this in the chapter Efficiency.

  • A lamp has no energy store. The energy that does not become light becomes internal energy of the lamp and its surroundings at once. Review this in the chapter Efficiency.

2. Water for tea 2 points

In a kettle $1.50\,\text{kg}$ of water is heated from $12.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ to $85.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ for tea. The specific heat capacity of water is $4190\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{kg}\cdot\text{K}}$.

How much heat is transferred to the water? Answer in kilojoules.

kJ
Hint 1: a guiding question

Does the state of the water change? Which law applies then?

Hint 2: what is the connection

Calculate the change in temperature: $85.0\,{}^\circ\text{C} - 12.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$.

Hint 3: an intermediate step

$$ \begin{aligned} Q &= 4190\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{kg}\cdot\text{K}} \cdot 1.50\,\text{kg} \cdot 73.0\,\text{K} \end{aligned} $$$$ \begin{aligned} Q &= 4190\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{kg}\cdot\text{K}} \cdot 1.50\,\text{kg} \\ &\cdot 73.0\,\text{K} \end{aligned} $$

Show the solution

Given

$m = 1.50\,\text{kg}$, $T_1 = 12.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, $T_2 = 85.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, $c = 4190\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{kg}\cdot\text{K}}$

Find

the heat $Q$ transferred to the water in kilojoules

Principle

The water warms up and its state does not change, because it does not boil. So the heat is proportional to the mass and to the change in temperature.

Formula

$Q = cm\Delta T$, where $\Delta T = T_2 - T_1$

Conversions

A temperature difference is the same in kelvins and in degrees Celsius:

$\Delta T$$= 85.0\,{}^\circ\text{C} - 12.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$
$= 73.0\,\text{K}$

Finally $1\,\text{kJ} = 1000\,\text{J}$.

Substitution

$$ \begin{aligned} Q &= 4190\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{kg}\cdot\text{K}} \cdot 1.50\,\text{kg} \cdot 73.0\,\text{K} \\ &= 458\,805\,\text{J} \\ &= 458.805\,\text{kJ} \end{aligned} $$$$ \begin{aligned} Q &= 4190\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{kg}\cdot\text{K}} \cdot 1.50\,\text{kg} \\ &\cdot 73.0\,\text{K} \\ &= 458\,805\,\text{J} \\ &= 458.805\,\text{kJ} \end{aligned} $$

Answer

$Q \approx 459\,\text{kJ}$

Three significant figures like the mass and the temperature difference.

Reasonableness

Roughly $$ 4000 \cdot 1.5 \cdot 75\,\text{J} = 450\,\text{kJ}\text{,}$$ close to the result. A $2\,\text{kW}$ kettle would give this energy in about four minutes, which fits experience.

3. Find the mistake: a warm bottle 2 points

A student calculated how high the pressure of the air in a closed glass bottle rises when the bottle warms in the sun from $20\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ to $60\,{}^\circ\text{C}$.At the start the pressure was $100\,\text{kPa}$: “$p_2 = p_1 \cdot \tfrac{T_2}{T_1} = 100\,\text{kPa} \cdot \tfrac{60}{20} = 300\,\text{kPa}$.” What is wrong in the solution?

  • In a rigid bottle the pressure increases with the temperature, because the particles move faster. The formula is correct; the mistake is that the temperatures are in degrees Celsius.

  • Correct. $$ \begin{aligned} p_2 &= 100\,\text{kPa} \cdot \tfrac{333\,\text{K}}{293\,\text{K}} \\ &\approx 114\,\text{kPa}\text{.} \end{aligned} $$ The proportionality starts from absolute zero, so the ratio is calculated from kelvins.

  • The units cancel, but the ratio of degrees Celsius $60 : 20$ is not the real ratio of the temperatures, because the zero of the Celsius scale is not absolute zero. In kelvins the ratio is only $333 : 293$.

4. Power 2 points

A washing machine uses $0.90\,\text{kWh}$ of electrical energy during one programme. The programme lasts $1\,\text{h}\ 30\,\text{min}$. Calculate the average power of the washing machine in kilowatts.

kW
Hint 1: a guiding question

Which quantity is energy divided by time?

Hint 2: what is the connection

How many hours is $1\,\text{h}\ 30\,\text{min}$? Not $1.30\,\text{h}$.

Hint 3: an intermediate step
$P$$= \dfrac{0.90\,\text{kWh}}{1.5\,\text{h}}$
Show the solution

Given

energy $E = 0.90\,\text{kWh}$, time $t = 1\,\text{h}\ 30\,\text{min}$

Find

the average power $P$ in kilowatts

Principle

Power tells how much energy is converted per unit time. The machine does not draw the same power all the time, so the energy divided by the whole time gives the average power.

Formula

$P = \dfrac{E}{t}$

Conversions

$30\,\text{min} = 0.5\,\text{h}$, so $t = 1.5\,\text{h}$. The energy is already in kilowatt-hours, so the result comes straight out in kilowatts.

Substitution

$P$$= \dfrac{0.90\,\text{kWh}}{1.5\,\text{h}}$
$= 0.60\,\text{kW}$

Answer

$P \approx 0.60\,\text{kW}$

Two significant figures like the energy $0.90\,\text{kWh}$.

Reasonableness

$0.60\,\text{kW}$ is $600\,\text{W}$. The heating element of a washing machine draws about $2\,\text{kW}$ for a while, but most of the time only the drum turns, so the average is much smaller. Check: $$ 0.60\,\text{kW} \cdot 1.5\,\text{h} = 0.90\,\text{kWh}\text{.}$$

5. Bath water 2 points

A bath holds $80.0\,\text{kg}$ of water at $42.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$. It is too hot, so cold water at $8.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ is added.

How much cold water is needed for the final temperature to be $37.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$? Heat losses are ignored. Give the answer in kilograms.

kg
Hint 1: a guiding question

The hot water cools and the cold water warms to the same final temperature. Which one gives out heat, and which one receives it?

Hint 2: what is the connection

Energy is conserved: $$ c m_1 (T_1 - T) = c m_2 (T - T_2)\text{.}$$ Which factor cancels?

Hint 3: an intermediate step
$m_2$$= \dfrac{m_1 (T_1 - T)}{T - T_2}$
$= \dfrac{80.0\,\text{kg} \cdot 5.0\,\text{K}}{29.0\,\text{K}}$
Show the solution

Given

hot water $m_1 = 80.0\,\text{kg}$, $T_1 = 42.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$; cold water $T_2 = 8.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$; final temperature $T = 37.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$

Find

the mass of the cold water $m_2$ in kilograms

Principle

Energy is conserved when there are no losses: the quantity of heat given out by the hot water equals the quantity received by the cold water. Each only cools or warms, so $Q = cm\Delta T$ holds, and $c$ is the same for both (chapter Heating and cooling of matter).

Formula

$$ c m_1 (T_1 - T) = c m_2 (T - T_2)\text{,}$$ so $m_2 = \dfrac{m_1 (T_1 - T)}{T - T_2}$

Conversions

None needed: the specific heat capacity cancels, and the temperature differences are the same in kelvins and in degrees Celsius.

Substitution

$m_2$$= \dfrac{80.0\,\text{kg} \cdot (42.0\,{}^\circ\text{C} - 37.0\,{}^\circ\text{C})}{37.0\,{}^\circ\text{C} - 8.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}}$
$= \dfrac{80.0\,\text{kg} \cdot 5.0\,\text{K}}{29.0\,\text{K}}$
$= 13.79\,\text{kg}$

Answer

$14\,\text{kg}$

Two significant figures, because the cooling of the hot water $42.0\,{}^\circ\text{C} - 37.0\,{}^\circ\text{C} = 5.0\,\text{K}$ is, as a difference, known to only two figures.

Reasonableness

The quantities of heat are equal: $c \cdot 80.0\,\text{kg} \cdot 5.0\,\text{K}$ and $c \cdot 13.79\,\text{kg} \cdot 29.0\,\text{K}$, because $80.0 \cdot 5.0 = 400$ and $13.79 \cdot 29.0 \approx 400$. Little cold water is needed, because it warms almost six times more than the hot water cools.
