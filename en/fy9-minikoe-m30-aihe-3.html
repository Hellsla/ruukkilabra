FY9Mini exam 30 min: Motion and Interaction

FY9 · Exam mode

Mini exam 30 min: Motion and Interaction

Time 30 min, at most 10 points. Hints, feedback and model answers open when you end the exam.

1. Find the mistake: average speed 2 points

A cyclist rides $10\,\text{km}$ uphill at $20\,\text{km/h}$ and comes back down the same distance at $60\,\text{km/h}$. A student calculated the average speed: “$(20\,\text{km/h} + 60\,\text{km/h}) : 2 = 40\,\text{km/h}$.” What is wrong in the solution?

  • Correct. The uphill takes $10\,\text{km} : 20\,\text{km/h} = 0.50\,\text{h}$ and the downhill $10\,\text{km} : 60\,\text{km/h} \approx 0.17\,\text{h}$, in total $\tfrac{2}{3}\,\text{h}$. The slower speed is ridden for longer, so it weighs more.

  • The average velocity, the displacement divided by the time, is zero here, but the question is about the average speed, which is calculated from the distance travelled: $20\,\text{km} : \tfrac{2}{3}\,\text{h} = 30\,\text{km/h}$.

  • The mean of the speeds would do only if each speed were ridden for equally long. The uphill takes three times as long as the downhill.

2. A crane lowers a load 2 points

A construction crane lowers a $650\,\text{kg}$ concrete element vertically downwards. Before it reaches the ground the crane brakes: the element still moves downwards, but its speed decreases steadily, and the deceleration is $0.60\,\text{m/s}^2$.

How large is the tension in the cable during the braking? Answer in kilonewtons.

kN
Hint 1: a guiding question

In which direction does the acceleration point when a load moving downwards slows down?

Hint 2: what is the connection

The acceleration points upwards, so the net force points upwards: $T - mg = ma$, with upwards positive.

Hint 3: an intermediate step
$T$$= m(g + a)$
$= 650\,\text{kg} \cdot (9.81 + 0.60)\,\text{m/s}^2$
Show the solution

Given

mass $m = 650\,\text{kg}$, deceleration $0.60\,\text{m/s}^2$ (the load moves downwards and slows down), $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$

Find

the tension $T$ in the cable in kilonewtons

Principle

The element is chosen as the object. The Earth (weight) and the cable (tension) act on it. The velocity points downwards and decreases, so the acceleration points upwards, and by Newton's second law the net force also points upwards (chapter Newton's second law).

Free-body diagram

The element as a rectangle, positive direction upwards. The weight $\overline{G}$ from the centre downwards and the tension of the cable $\overline{T}$ from the top surface upwards, longer than the weight. The downward velocity and the upward acceleration are marked separately.

Formula

$T - mg = ma$, so $T = m(g + a)$

Conversions

The acceleration points upwards, so $a = +0.60\,\text{m/s}^2$. Finally $1\,\text{N} = 0.001\,\text{kN}$.

Substitution

$$ \begin{aligned} T &= 650\,\text{kg} \cdot (9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 + 0.60\,\text{m/s}^2) \\ &= 650\,\text{kg} \cdot 10.41\,\text{m/s}^2 \\ &= 6766.5\,\text{N} \\ &= 6.7665\,\text{kN} \end{aligned} $$$$ \begin{aligned} T &= 650\,\text{kg} \\ &\cdot (9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 + 0.60\,\text{m/s}^2) \\ &= 650\,\text{kg} \cdot 10.41\,\text{m/s}^2 \\ &= 6766.5\,\text{N} \\ &= 6.7665\,\text{kN} \end{aligned} $$

Answer

$6.8\,\text{kN}$

Two significant figures like the deceleration $0.60\,\text{m/s}^2$.

Reasonableness

The weight is $$ 650\,\text{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 \approx 6.38\,\text{kN}\text{.}$$ During braking the cable must both hold up the weight and slow the load down, so $T$ is about $6\,\%$ larger than the weight, as it should be when $a$ is about $6\,\%$ of $g$.

3. Find the mistake: a ball bounces off a wall 2 points

A student calculated the change in momentum of a $0.20\,\text{kg}$ ball that hits a wall at $5.0\,\text{m/s}$ and bounces straight back at $4.0\,\text{m/s}$: “$\Delta p = 0.20\,\text{kg} \cdot 4.0\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} - 0.20\,\text{kg} \cdot 5.0\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} = -0.20\,\text{kg} \cdot \text{m/s}$.” What is wrong in the solution?

  • Correct. When the direction towards the wall is positive, $$ \begin{aligned} \Delta p &= 0.20\,\text{kg} \cdot (-4.0\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}) - 0.20\,\text{kg} \cdot 5.0\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} \\ &= -1.8\,\text{kg} \cdot \text{m/s}\text{.} \end{aligned} $$$$ \begin{aligned} \Delta p &= 0.20\,\text{kg} \cdot (-4.0\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}) \\ &- 0.20\,\text{kg} \cdot 5.0\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} \\ &= -1.8\,\text{kg} \cdot \text{m/s}\text{.} \end{aligned} $$ The impulse the wall exerts on the ball is equal to it.

  • The momentum $p = mv$ changes when the velocity changes, and a mere change of direction also changes the velocity. The mistake is in the sign: the velocity of the ball bouncing back is $-4.0\,\text{m/s}$.

  • The change in momentum needs the mass, and its unit is $\text{kg} \cdot \text{m/s}$. The magnitude of the change in velocity is indeed $9.0\,\text{m/s}$ when the signs are taken into account, so $|\Delta p| = 0.20\,\text{kg} \cdot 9.0\,\text{m/s}$.

4. The moment on a pedal 2 points

A cyclist stands on a pedal and presses it straight down with a force of $520\,\text{N}$. The length of the crank from the bottom-bracket axle to the pedal axle is $0.170\,\text{m}$, and the crank points forwards $35^\circ$ above the horizontal.

How large is the moment of the force about the bottom-bracket axle?

Nm
Hint 1: a guiding question

The lever arm of the force is the perpendicular distance of the bottom-bracket axle from the line of action of the force. Draw the crank and the vertical line through the pedal.

Hint 2: what is the connection

The distance of the vertical line of action from the axle is the horizontal reach of the crank: $r = d\cos 35^\circ$.

Hint 3: an intermediate step

$$ M = 520\,\text{N} \cdot 0.170\,\text{m} \cdot \cos 35^\circ $$

Show the solution

Given

force $F = 520\,\text{N}$ straight down, length of the crank $d = 0.170\,\text{m}$, crank $35^\circ$ above the horizontal

Find

the moment $M$ of the force about the bottom-bracket axle

Principle

The crank can only turn about the bottom-bracket axle, so the turning effect of the force is given by the moment $M = Fr$. The lever arm $r$ is the perpendicular distance of the axle from the vertical line of action of the force. The angle between the force and the extension of the crank is $\alpha = 90^\circ + 35^\circ = 125^\circ$, so $r = d\sin 125^\circ = d\cos 35^\circ$ (chapter Moment: the turning effect of a force).

Formula

$M = Fr$ and $r = d\cos 35^\circ$, so $M = Fd\cos 35^\circ$

Conversions

None needed: the force is in newtons and the length in metres, so the moment comes out in newton-metres.

Substitution

$$ \begin{aligned} M &= 520\,\text{N} \cdot 0.170\,\text{m} \cdot \cos 35^\circ \\ &= 88.4\,\text{Nm} \cdot 0.8192 \\ &= 72.41\,\text{Nm} \end{aligned} $$

Answer

$M \approx 72\,\text{Nm}$

Two significant figures like the angle $35^\circ$.

Reasonableness

When the crank is horizontal, the lever arm is the whole $0.170\,\text{m}$ and the moment is largest, $88\,\text{Nm}$. When the crank is vertical, the lever arm is zero. The result lies between these, and at the angle $35^\circ$ the lever arm is still over $80\,\%$ of the length of the crank.

5. Braking 2 points

A car moving at $25.0\,\text{m/s}$ brakes uniformly and stops in a distance of $40.0\,\text{m}$.

How large is the deceleration of the car, that is the magnitude of its acceleration? Answer in the unit $\text{m/s}^2$.

m/s²
Hint 1: a guiding question

The time is not known. Which equation of uniformly accelerated motion does not contain the time?

Hint 2: what is the connection

$v^2 = v_0^2 + 2as$, and the final speed is zero.

Hint 3: an intermediate step
$a$$= -\dfrac{(25.0\,\text{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 40.0\,\text{m}}$
Show the solution

Given

initial speed $v_0 = 25.0\,\text{m/s}$, final speed $v = 0$, braking distance $s = 40.0\,\text{m}$; acceleration constant

Find

the magnitude of the acceleration $|a|$ in the unit $\text{m/s}^2$

Principle

The car brakes uniformly, so its acceleration is constant and the equations of uniformly accelerated motion hold. The time is not known, so the equation without it is used.

Formula

$v^2 = v_0^2 + 2as$, where $v = 0$ gives $a = -\dfrac{v_0^2}{2s}$

Conversions

None needed: the speed is in the unit $\text{m/s}$ and the distance in metres.

Substitution

$a$$= -\dfrac{(25.0\,\text{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 40.0\,\text{m}}$
$= -\dfrac{625\,\text{m}^2/\text{s}^2}{80.0\,\text{m}}$
$= -7.8125\,\text{m/s}^2$

Answer

$|a| \approx 7.81\,\text{m/s}^2$

Three significant figures like the speed and the distance. The minus sign says that the acceleration is against the motion.

Reasonableness

The deceleration is about $0.8\,g$, which is possible in an emergency stop on dry asphalt. The braking time would be $\tfrac{25.0}{7.81}\,\text{s} \approx 3.2\,\text{s}$, and the average speed $12.5\,\text{m/s}$ times $3.2\,\text{s}$ gives the distance $40\,\text{m}$.
