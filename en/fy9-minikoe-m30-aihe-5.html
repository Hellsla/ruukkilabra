FY9Mini exam 30 min: Electricity and Magnetism

FY9 · Exam mode

Mini exam 30 min: Electricity and Magnetism

Time 30 min, at most 10 points. Hints, feedback and model answers open when you end the exam.

1. Meters 2 points

A student wants to measure the electric current through a desk lamp and the voltage across the lamp's terminals with multimeters. How are the meters connected?

  • The resistance of a voltmeter is very large. Connected in series it almost stops the current, and the lamp does not light. The voltmeter is connected in parallel with the lamp. Review this in the chapter Measurements in an electric circuit.

  • An ammeter connected in parallel short-circuits the lamp. The ammeter is in series and the voltmeter in parallel; that is exactly why each changes the circuit only a little. Review this in the chapter Measurements in an electric circuit.

  • Correct. The same current as through the lamp must flow through the ammeter, so it is in series. The voltmeter measures the potential difference between two points, so it is connected in parallel across the lamp's terminals. Review this in the chapter Measurements in an electric circuit.

  • The resistance of an ammeter is very small. Connected in parallel it short-circuits the lamp, and the large current may damage the meter. The ammeter belongs in series. Review this in the chapter Measurements in an electric circuit.

2. Three charges on a rail 2 points

Three small charged balls are on an insulating rail on the same line. Ball A, whose charge is $+4.0\,\text{nC}$, is at $0\,\text{cm}$, ball B, whose charge is $-6.0\,\text{nC}$, is at $5.0\,\text{cm}$ and ball C, whose charge is $+2.0\,\text{nC}$, is at $10.0\,\text{cm}$.

How large is the total electric force on ball C?

µN
Hint 1: a guiding question

Calculate the forces of A and B on ball C separately with Coulomb's law. Remember the distances to ball C: $10.0\,\text{cm}$ and $5.0\,\text{cm}$.

Hint 2: what is the connection

A and C have the same sign, so A pushes C away, to the right. B and C have opposite signs, so B pulls C to the left. Forces in opposite directions are subtracted.

Hint 3: an intermediate step
$F_\text{A}$$= k\dfrac{4.0 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{C} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{C}}{(0.100\,\text{m})^2}$
$F_\text{B}$$= k\dfrac{6.0 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{C} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{C}}{(0.050\,\text{m})^2}$
Show the solution

Given

$q_\text{A} = +4.0\,\text{nC}$ at $0\,\text{cm}$, $q_\text{B} = -6.0\,\text{nC}$ at $5.0\,\text{cm}$, $q_\text{C} = +2.0\,\text{nC}$ at $10.0\,\text{cm}$, $k = 8.988 \cdot 10^{9}\,\tfrac{\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2}{\text{C}^2}$

Find

the magnitude $F$ of the total force on ball C

Principle

The balls are small, so they are point charges. Each of the other balls acts on C as if the other one were not there, and the total force is the vector sum of the forces. The positive direction is chosen to the right. A and C have the same sign, so the force of A points away from C, that is, to the right. B and C have opposite signs, so the force of B points towards B, that is, to the left (chapter Electric charge and Coulomb's law).

Formula

$F_\text{A}$$= k\dfrac{\lvert q_\text{A} q_\text{C} \rvert}{r_\text{AC}^2}$
$F_\text{B}$$= k\dfrac{\lvert q_\text{B} q_\text{C} \rvert}{r_\text{BC}^2}$

$F = F_\text{A} - F_\text{B}$

Conversions

$r_\text{AC}$$= 10.0\,\text{cm}$
$= 0.100\,\text{m}$
$r_\text{BC}$$= 5.0\,\text{cm}$
$= 0.050\,\text{m}$

$1\,\text{nC} = 10^{-9}\,\text{C}$. Finally $1\,\text{N} = 10^{6}\,\mu\text{N}$.

Substitution

$$ \begin{aligned} F_\text{A} &= 8.988 \cdot 10^{9}\,\tfrac{\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2}{\text{C}^2} \cdot \dfrac{8.0 \cdot 10^{-18}\,\text{C}^2}{0.0100\,\text{m}^2} \\ &= 7.19 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{N}\text{,} \end{aligned} $$$$ \begin{aligned} F_\text{A} &= 8.988 \cdot 10^{9}\,\tfrac{\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2}{\text{C}^2} \\ &\cdot \dfrac{8.0 \cdot 10^{-18}\,\text{C}^2}{0.0100\,\text{m}^2} \\ &= 7.19 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{N}\text{,} \end{aligned} $$ $$ \begin{aligned} F_\text{B} &= 8.988 \cdot 10^{9}\,\tfrac{\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2}{\text{C}^2} \cdot \dfrac{12 \cdot 10^{-18}\,\text{C}^2}{0.0025\,\text{m}^2} \\ &= 43.14 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{N} \end{aligned} $$$$ \begin{aligned} F_\text{B} &= 8.988 \cdot 10^{9}\,\tfrac{\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2}{\text{C}^2} \\ &\cdot \dfrac{12 \cdot 10^{-18}\,\text{C}^2}{0.0025\,\text{m}^2} \\ &= 43.14 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{N} \end{aligned} $$ and

$F$$= 7.19\,\mu\text{N} - 43.14\,\mu\text{N}$
$= -35.95\,\mu\text{N}$

Answer

$F \approx 36\,\mu\text{N}$

To the left, towards ball B. Two significant figures, as in the charges.

Reasonableness

The force of B is larger, because B is half as far away and its charge is larger: the distance alone makes the force four times as large and the charge one and a half times as large, six times in total. That is why the total force points towards B.

3. Induction 2 points

A bar magnet has been pushed into a coil and is held still there. A voltmeter connected to the ends of the coil reads zero.Which statement is correct?

  • Induction does not need a battery: a changing magnetic flux induces the voltage by itself. Here the flux does not change, so the voltage is zero. Review this in the chapter Electromagnetic induction.

  • Correct. By the law of induction the induced voltage is proportional to the rate of change of the magnetic flux. A magnet held still gives a constant flux, so the voltage is zero. Review this in the chapter Electromagnetic induction.

  • Even a strong magnet held still gives a constant flux, and no voltage is induced. What matters is the change of the flux, not its size. Review this in the chapter Electromagnetic induction.

  • The magnet does not short-circuit the coil. The voltage is zero because the magnetic flux does not change. Move the magnet, and the meter needle swings. Review this in the chapter Electromagnetic induction.

4. Parallel connection 2 points

Two resistors, $120\,\Omega$ and $180\,\Omega$, are connected in parallel. How large is the total resistance of the connection?

Ω
Hint 1: a guiding question

What is the same for resistors in parallel, and how do the currents combine?

Hint 2: what is the connection
$\dfrac{1}{R}$$= \dfrac{1}{R_1} + \dfrac{1}{R_2}$

: remember to take the reciprocal at the end.

Hint 3: an intermediate step
$R$$= \dfrac{120\,\Omega \cdot 180\,\Omega}{300\,\Omega}$
Show the solution

Given

$R_1 = 120\,\Omega$, $R_2 = 180\,\Omega$, connected in parallel

Find

the total resistance $R$ in ohms

Principle

In a parallel connection the resistors have the same voltage, and the total current is the sum of the branch currents. So the reciprocals of the resistances add up.

Formula

$\dfrac{1}{R}$$= \dfrac{1}{R_1} + \dfrac{1}{R_2}$

so $R = \dfrac{R_1 R_2}{R_1 + R_2}$

Conversions

None needed: both resistances are in ohms.

Substitution

$R$$= \dfrac{120\,\Omega \cdot 180\,\Omega}{120\,\Omega + 180\,\Omega}$
$= \dfrac{21\,600\,\Omega^2}{300\,\Omega}$
$= 72.0\,\Omega$

Answer

$R \approx 72.0\,\Omega$

Three significant figures like the resistances.

Reasonableness

The resistance of a parallel connection is smaller than the smallest single resistance $120\,\Omega$, because the current gets a new path. Check:

$\tfrac{1}{120} + \tfrac{1}{180}$$= \tfrac{5}{360}$
$= \tfrac{1}{72}$

5. A dust particle in an electrostatic precipitator 2 points

The two parallel plates of an electrostatic precipitator are $8.0\,\text{mm}$ apart, and the voltage between them is $6.0\,\text{kV}$. A dust particle has received a charge of $50$ elementary charges.

How large is the electric force on the particle between the plates?

pN
Hint 1: a guiding question

The field between the plates is uniform. First calculate the field strength from the voltage and the distance between the plates.

Hint 2: what is the connection

$E = \tfrac{U}{d}$ and $F = qE$, where $q = 50e$.

Hint 3: an intermediate step

$$ \begin{aligned} F &= 50 \cdot 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{C} \cdot \dfrac{6000\,\text{V}}{0.0080\,\text{m}}\text{,} \end{aligned} $$$$ \begin{aligned} F &= 50 \cdot 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{C} \\ &\cdot \dfrac{6000\,\text{V}}{0.0080\,\text{m}}\text{,} \end{aligned} $$ and finally $1\,\text{pN} = 10^{-12}\,\text{N}$.

Show the solution

Given

the distance between the plates $d = 8.0\,\text{mm}$, the voltage $U = 6.0\,\text{kV}$, the charge $q = 50e$, $e = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{C}$

Find

the electric force $F$ on the particle

Principle

Between large parallel plates the field is uniform, so its strength is the voltage divided by the distance between the plates, and the force is the same everywhere between the plates. The charge consists of elementary charges (chapters Uniform electric field and Electric charge and Coulomb's law).

Formula

$E = \dfrac{U}{d}$ and $F = qE = 50e \cdot \dfrac{U}{d}$

Conversions

$U$$= 6.0\,\text{kV}$
$= 6.0 \cdot 1000\,\text{V}$
$= 6000\,\text{V}$
$d$$= 8.0\,\text{mm}$
$= 8.0 \cdot 0.001\,\text{m}$
$= 0.0080\,\text{m}$

Finally $1\,\text{N} = 10^{12}\,\text{pN}$.

Substitution

$E$$= \dfrac{6000\,\text{V}}{0.0080\,\text{m}}$
$= 7.5 \cdot 10^{5}\,\text{V/m}$

$$ \begin{aligned} F &= 50 \cdot 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{C} \cdot 7.5 \cdot 10^{5}\,\text{V/m} \\ &= 6.008 \cdot 10^{-12}\,\text{N} \\ &= 6.0\,\text{pN} \end{aligned} $$$$ \begin{aligned} F &= 50 \cdot 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{C} \cdot 7.5 \\ &\cdot 10^{5}\,\text{V/m} \\ &= 6.008 \cdot 10^{-12}\,\text{N} \\ &= 6.0\,\text{pN} \end{aligned} $$

Answer

$F \approx 6.0\,\text{pN}$

Two significant figures, as in the voltage and the distance.

Reasonableness

The field $0.75\,\text{MV/m}$ is below the dielectric strength of air, $3\,\text{MV/m}$, so the precipitator works without sparking. The force is small, but the mass of a dust particle a micrometre in size is only about $10^{-15}\,\text{kg}$, so its weight is about $10^{-14}\,\text{N}$, hundreds of times smaller than the electric force. That is why the field steers the particle onto the plate.
