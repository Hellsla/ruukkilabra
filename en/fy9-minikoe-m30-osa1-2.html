Mini exam 30 min: Part I (2)

Time 30 min, at most 10 points. Hints, feedback and model answers open when you end the exam.

  • Answer 5 of the 6 items: choose them before you answer.

1. The line of a spring 2 points

Different weights were hung from a spring, and with each load the total length $L$ of the spring was measured. The $(L, F)$ line fitted to the points passes through the points $(15.0\,\text{cm}$; $1.2\,\text{N})$ and $(35.0\,\text{cm}$; $17.0\,\text{N})$, where $F$ is the force stretching the spring.

What is the spring constant of the spring? Answer in the unit $\text{N/m}$.

N/m
Hint 1: a guiding question

The extension of the spring is $L - L_0$, where $L_0$ is the length of the spring without a load. Which quantity of the line is the spring constant: the slope or the constant term?

Hint 2: what is the connection

$F = k(L - L_0) = kL - kL_0$, so the spring constant is the slope $k = \dfrac{\Delta F}{\Delta L}$.

Hint 3: an intermediate step
$k$$= \dfrac{17.0\,\text{N} - 1.2\,\text{N}}{0.350\,\text{m} - 0.150\,\text{m}}$
Show the solution

Given

points of the fitted line $(L_1$; $F_1) = (15.0\,\text{cm}$; $1.2\,\text{N})$ and $(L_2$; $F_2) = (35.0\,\text{cm}$; $17.0\,\text{N})$; $L$ is the total length of the spring

Find

the spring constant $k$ in the unit $\text{N/m}$

Principle

The extension of the spring is $L - L_0$, where $L_0$ is the length of the spring without a load, and the stretching force is proportional to the extension. Then $F = kL - kL_0$ is a straight line whose slope is the spring constant and whose constant term is $-kL_0$. The ratio $F : L$ is not the spring constant, because $L$ is not the extension.

Formula

$k$$= \dfrac{\Delta F}{\Delta L}$
$= \dfrac{F_2 - F_1}{L_2 - L_1}$

Conversions

$L_1$$= 15.0\,\text{cm}$
$= 15.0 \cdot 0.01\,\text{m}$
$= 0.150\,\text{m}$
$L_2$$= 35.0 \cdot 0.01\,\text{m}$
$= 0.350\,\text{m}$

Substitution

$k$$= \dfrac{17.0\,\text{N} - 1.2\,\text{N}}{0.350\,\text{m} - 0.150\,\text{m}}$
$= \dfrac{15.8\,\text{N}}{0.200\,\text{m}}$
$= 79.0\,\tfrac{\text{N}}{\text{m}}$

Answer

$k \approx 79.0\,\text{N/m}$

Three significant figures as in the differences $15.8\,\text{N}$ and $0.200\,\text{m}$.

Reasonableness

The length of the spring without a load is

$L_0$$= L_1 - \tfrac{F_1}{k}$
$= 0.150\,\text{m} - \tfrac{1.2\,\text{N}}{79.0\,\text{N/m}}$
$\approx 0.135\,\text{m}$

which is shorter than any measured length, as it should be. A spring constant of $79\,\text{N/m}$ is of the usual order of magnitude for a school extension spring.

2. Melting ice 2 points

A glass holds a mixture of ice and water at $0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$. The glass is heated slowly and the mixture is stirred. The temperature stays at $0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ as long as there is ice left.Why?

  • Correct. During melting the energy transferred to the substance changes the interactions between the particles and increases the interaction energy between them. The temperature starts to rise only when all the ice has melted. Review this in the chapter Changes of state.

  • The temperature stays constant however long you measure. A pure substance melts at constant pressure at a constant temperature, because the energy goes into the change of state. Review this in the chapter Changes of state.

  • In melting the molecules do not break apart; the interactions between the water molecules change. The energy increases the interaction energy between the particles. Review this in the chapter Changes of state.

  • When the mixture is heated, energy is transferred to it: the ice keeps melting. The energy goes into the change of state, so the temperature does not rise. Review this in the chapter Changes of state.

3. A collision where the cars stick together 2 points

On a slippery road a car of mass $1250\,\text{kg}$ moving at $15.0\,\text{m/s}$ hits a stationary $850\,\text{kg}$ car. The cars stick together.

How large is their common speed just after the collision? The friction of the tyres is neglected during the short collision.

m/s
Hint 1: a guiding question

Which quantity is conserved in a short collision when the external forces are small?

Hint 2: what is the connection

Before the collision only the moving car has momentum; afterwards the cars move together.

Hint 3: an intermediate step
$v'$$= \dfrac{1250\,\text{kg} \cdot 15.0\,\text{m/s}}{2100\,\text{kg}}$
Show the solution

Given

$m_1 = 1250\,\text{kg}$, $v_1 = 15.0\,\text{m/s}$, $m_2 = 850\,\text{kg}$, $v_2 = 0$; the cars stick together

Find

the common speed $v'$ just after the collision

Principle

The collision lasts a short time, and the external horizontal forces, such as the friction of the tyres, are small compared with the forces between the cars. So the total momentum of the cars is conserved. Kinetic energy is not conserved, because the cars are deformed.

Formula

$$ m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2 = (m_1 + m_2)v'\text{,}$$ so $v' = \dfrac{m_1 v_1}{m_1 + m_2}$ when $v_2 = 0$

Conversions

None needed: the masses are in kilograms and the speed in the unit $\text{m/s}$.

Substitution

$v'$$= \dfrac{1250\,\text{kg} \cdot 15.0\,\text{m/s}}{1250\,\text{kg} + 850\,\text{kg}}$
$= \dfrac{18\,750\,\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}}{2100\,\text{kg}}$
$= 8.9286\,\text{m/s}$

Answer

$v' \approx 8.93\,\text{m/s}$

Three significant figures like the speed $15.0\,\text{m/s}$.

Reasonableness

The common speed is smaller than the speed of the moving car, because the same momentum is shared by a larger mass. The mass becomes $1.68$ times larger, and the speed decreases in the same ratio: $15.0 : 1.68 \approx 8.9$.

4. Read the graph: one point of a rope 2 points

The graph shows the displacement of one point of a wave travelling along a rope as a function of time. The wave travels along the rope at $3.0\,\text{m/s}$.Which statement is correct?

  • Correct. The curve rises through the axis every $0.40\,\text{s}$, so $T = 0.40\,\text{s}$ and $f = \tfrac{1}{T} = 2.5\,\text{Hz}$. The wavelength is $\lambda = \tfrac{v}{f} = \tfrac{3.0\,\text{m/s}}{2.5\,\text{Hz}} = 1.2\,\text{m}$.

  • Successive crossings of the axis are half a period apart, because the point moves in opposite directions at them. The period is the interval between two upward crossings, $0.40\,\text{s}$. Review in the chapter Describing oscillation: period, frequency and amplitude.

  • The horizontal axis shows time, so the interval $0.40\,\text{s}$ between the peaks is the period, not the wavelength. The wavelength follows from the speed: $\lambda = vT = 1.2\,\text{m}$. Review in the chapter How waves form and travel.

5. Refraction of light 2 points

A ray of light comes from air to the surface of water at an angle of incidence of $40.0^\circ$ to the normal of the surface. The refractive index of water is $1.33$ and that of air $1.00$.

How large is the angle of refraction?

°
Hint 1: a guiding question

In which direction does light bend when it slows down?

Hint 2: what is the connection

The law of refraction: $n_1 \sin\alpha = n_2 \sin\beta$.

Hint 3: an intermediate step
$\sin\beta$$= \dfrac{\sin 40.0^\circ}{1.33}$

and the angle comes from the inverse function on the calculator.

Show the solution

Given

angle of incidence $\alpha = 40.0^\circ$, refractive index of air $n_1 = 1.00$, of water $n_2 = 1.33$

Find

the angle of refraction $\beta$ in degrees from the normal

Principle

Light slows down when it passes from air into water, so it bends towards the normal at the boundary. The law of refraction connects the angles of incidence and refraction.

Formula

$n_1 \sin\alpha = n_2 \sin\beta$, so

$\sin\beta$$= \dfrac{n_1 \sin\alpha}{n_2}$

Conversions

None needed: the angles are in degrees, and refractive indices have no unit.

Substitution

$\sin\beta$$= \dfrac{1.00 \cdot \sin 40.0^\circ}{1.33}$
$= \dfrac{0.64279}{1.33}$
$= 0.48330$

so $\beta = 28.901^\circ$

Answer

$\beta \approx 28.9^\circ$

Three significant figures like the angle of incidence and the refractive index.

Reasonableness

The angle of refraction is smaller than the angle of incidence, because light bends towards the normal in an optically denser medium.

6. Find the mistake: the photons of a laser pointer 2 points

The power of a green laser pointer is $5.0\,\text{mW}$ and its wavelength $532\,\text{nm}$.A student calculated the photon energy $\tfrac{hc}{\lambda} = 2.33\,\text{eV}$ and wrote: “The number of photons leaving per second is $\tfrac{P}{E} = \tfrac{5.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\text{W}}{2.33\,\text{eV}} = 2.1 \cdot 10^{-3}$.” What is wrong in the solution?

  • Correct. $$ \begin{aligned} 2.33\,\text{eV} &= 2.33 \cdot 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J} \\ &= 3.73 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}\text{,} \end{aligned} $$$$ \begin{aligned} &2.33\,\text{eV} \\ &= 2.33 \cdot 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J} \\ &= 3.73 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}\text{,} \end{aligned} $$ so $\tfrac{N}{t} = \tfrac{5.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\text{W}}{3.73 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}} \approx 1.3 \cdot 10^{16}\,\tfrac{1}{\text{s}}$. Review if needed in the chapter Lasers and quantum structures.

  • The laser power $5.0\,\text{mW}$ is already the power of the light. The mistake is that the energy is in electronvolts: it must be converted into joules before dividing. Review in the chapter Lasers and quantum structures.

  • The expressions are the same, because $f = \tfrac{c}{\lambda}$, and the energy $2.33\,\text{eV}$ is right. The mistake is in the units: the ratio of watts to electronvolts is not a number per second. Review in the chapter Lasers and quantum structures.
